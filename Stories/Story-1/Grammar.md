## Story 1: The Unexpected Shortcut

| Grammar Point                      | Rule / Structure                                                | Example from the Story                             | Additional Example                           |
| ---------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Past Simple                        | Subject + past verb                                             | Mark had a list of errands.                        | I finished my work yesterday.                |
| Past Continuous                    | Subject + was/were + verb-ing                                   | Rain was coming down in buckets.                   | I was driving to work.                       |
| Past Continuous + Past Simple      | An ongoing action interrupted by another action                 | His car hit a huge pothole.                        | I was sleeping when the phone rang.          |
| Past Perfect                       | Subject + had + past participle                                 | A road he had never been on before.                | I had never visited London before.           |
| Past Simple for a Sequence         | Use past simple to describe events in order                     | He turned onto a small side street.                | She opened the door and walked inside.       |
| Past Continuous for Background     | Use past continuous to describe the background of a story       | It was a gloomy Saturday morning.                  | The sun was shining, and birds were singing. |
| Have to + Base Verb                | Have to + verb expresses necessity or obligation                | He had to pick up a birthday cake.                 | I have to finish this report today.          |
| Be Going to                        | Subject + am/is/are + going to + verb                           | He was going to be late.                           | I'm going to study English tonight.          |
| Decide + To-Infinitive             | Decide + to + base verb                                         | He decided to take a shortcut.                     | I decided to learn JavaScript.               |
| Start + To-Infinitive / Verb-ing   | Both forms can follow start                                     | He started to laugh.                               | It started raining.                          |
| Because + Clause                   | Because + subject + verb explains a reason                      | He was in a jam because of the traffic.            | I stayed home because it was raining.        |
| Because of + Noun                  | Because of + noun or noun phrase                                | He was late because of the traffic.                | The match was canceled because of the rain.  |
| Adjective + Noun                   | An adjective describes a noun                                   | A gloomy morning.                                  | A difficult problem.                         |
| Adverb of Manner                   | Many adverbs describe how an action happens                     | He was really making good time.                    | She spoke slowly.                            |
| Relative Clause                    | Who / which / that + clause gives more information about a noun | A road he had never been on before.                | This is the book that I bought.              |
| Have to vs. Had to                 | Have to = present/future necessity; had to = past necessity     | He had to pick up a cake.                          | I had to work late yesterday.                |
| Would / Will for Decisions         | Will can express a decision made at the moment of speaking      | "I'll take a shortcut," he decided.                | I'll call you when I arrive.                 |
| Imperative                         | Base verb at the beginning gives an instruction                 | Read the story again.                              | Listen carefully.                            |
| Try to + Verb                      | Try to + base verb describes an attempt                         | Try to pay attention to the flow.                  | I try to speak English every day.            |
| Without + Verb-ing                 | Without + gerund describes an action that does not happen       | Deal with a situation without getting too upset.   | He left without saying goodbye.              |
| Have to + Verb / Just Have to      | Expresses necessity; context determines how strong it is        | Sometimes you just have to roll with the punches.  | Sometimes you just have to be patient.       |
| It was + Adjective + To-Infinitive | It + be + adjective + to + verb evaluates an action             | It might feel weird at first.                      | It is important to practice daily.           |
| How Much + Uncountable Noun        | Ask about an amount of something uncountable                    | See how much we can learn.                         | How much time do you need?                   |
| Every Single + Singular Noun       | Adds emphasis to each individual item                           | Don't worry about understanding every single word. | I reviewed every single sentence.            |

##### Important Notes

- **Past Simple:** Use it for completed past events.

- **Past Continuous:** Use it for actions in progress at a past time or for background descriptions.

- **Past Perfect:** Use it when you need to show that one past event happened before another past event.

- **Because vs. Because of:** Use *because* before a clause and *because of* before a noun or noun phrase.

- **Without + verb-ing:** After the preposition *without*, use a noun or a gerund (verb-ing).

- **Will:** Use it for spontaneous decisions, predictions, and promises, among other functions.
